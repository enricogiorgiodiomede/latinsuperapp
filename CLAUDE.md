# Latin Authors Tier List — Project Instructions

## Overview

This project produces a long-form reference document covering Roman authors from Livius Andronicus to Boethius. Each entry profiles an author's literary output, writing style, and translation difficulty, including original Latin excerpts with Italian and English translations.

The project targets an intermediate Latin student audience (Liceo Scientifico level) and uses an accessible, occasionally humorous tone. The end goal is a YouTube-ready series, so entries should be engaging, not dry.

**Always ask permission before starting work on a new entry or era.**

---

## Output Format

- **Format:** Google Docs (one document per era)
- **Eras (5 documents):**
  1. Archaic Era (III century BC -- beginning of I century BC)
  2. Caesar's Era (100--44 BC)
  3. Augustan Era (44 BC -- 14 AD)
  4. Early Imperial Era (I--II century AD)
  5. Late Imperial Era (II century AD -- 476 AD + Boethius)

## Era Introduction

Each era document opens with an introductory section covering:
- Historical and political context of the period
- Cultural context (especially Rome's relationship with Greece, which dominates the Archaic and early Classical eras)
- Stylistic overview: what characterizes the literature of this era, what models authors worked from, what forms and meters were in use
- State of the surviving corpus (fragmentary vs. well-preserved)

Keep the focus on style above all. For example, the Archaic Era introduction should explain how the first Latin works are almost entirely adaptations or imitations of Greek models, and what that means for the nature of the texts.

---

## Entry Structure

Each author entry follows this structure, in order:

### 1. Biography
- **Mainstream authors** (e.g. Caesar, Cicero): Keep it concise but substantial enough for a reader to understand who this person was and why they matter. Always include funny anecdotes or quirky facts where available.
- **Lesser-known authors with very scarce fragments** (e.g. Caecilius Statius): The biography takes up a larger share of the entry since there is less literary material to discuss. Still include the Latin excerpt and translation -- do not skip them.

### 2. Most Important Works
A brief, selective list -- not exhaustive. Focus on works the reader has a realistic chance of encountering. Note if a work is lost or survives only in fragments.

### 3. Writing Style and Difficulty
Describe the author's style and explain the main reasons for difficulty. Use the ranking criteria (see below) as the analytical backbone, but write in flowing prose, not as a checklist. Humor is welcome here.

### 4. Latin Excerpt + Translation + Analysis
- **Latin text:** Sourced from an official website (The Latin Library or Splash Latino). Reproduce it in Latin exactly as found.
- **Italian translation:** Original rendering. Must NOT be copied from any official website. Aim for readable, natural Italian.
- **English translation:** Original rendering. Must NOT be copied from any official website. Aim for readable, natural English.
- **Analysis:** Comment on the passage's context (what is happening, why this passage was chosen) and its structural features (syntax, meter, key vocabulary, stylistic figures).

### 5. Tier Ranking
Assign a tier based on the criteria below. State the tier, then briefly justify it by referencing the relevant criteria. For borderline cases, flag the ambiguity explicitly.

## Tier System

Use the `latin-tier-list` skill for all ranking logic. The tiers are:

| Tier | Label | Description |
|------|-------|-------------|
| S | GOD PLEASE HELP | Maximum difficulty across multiple criteria simultaneously |
| A | Very Difficult | High difficulty in at least two major criteria |
| B | Difficult | Demanding but approachable with preparation |
| C | Manageable | Accessible with standard Latin training |
| D | Good Exercise | Simple, clear, good for consolidation |
| NC | Not Comparable | Surviving material too fragmentary to rank reliably |

### Ranking Criteria (summary)
1. **Lexicon** -- archaic forms, technical vocabulary, hapax legomena
2. **Medium** -- poetry is harder than prose; within each, apply the difficulty ladder from the skill
3. **Syntactic complexity** -- brevitas, periodic sentences, variatio, hyperbaton, oratio obliqua
4. **Stylistic figures** -- ellipsis, asyndeton, synchysis, irony
5. **Allusion density** -- unmarked allusions to Greek models, obscure myths, political subtext
6. **Genre and subject matter** -- philosophy, satire, epic, comedy each add specific burdens

For fragmentary authors: scale the tier down approximately one step from intrinsic per-line difficulty. When surviving material is extremely sparse (a handful of isolated lines with no complete work), NC is the appropriate call even if the per-line difficulty is assessable.

Invoke the `latin-tier-list` skill when working on any ranking to apply the full criteria consistently and update the rankings table.

## Tone

- Accessible and engaging; suitable for a Liceo Scientifico student and a general YouTube audience.
- Humor, jokes, and quirky facts are encouraged, especially in biography sections and when describing stylistic peculiarities.
- Avoid dry academic register. The goal is to make readers want to keep reading.

---

## Language Rules

- Document text: English throughout.
- Latin excerpts: always in Latin, sourced from official sites.
- Each excerpt section includes two translations: Italian first, then English. Both must be original compositions, not copied from official sources.
- The Italian translation is kept because the user plans to adapt it independently.
- Do not use em dashes. Use regular dashes or restructure sentences instead.

---

## Workflow

1. Confirm permission before starting each era or author.
2. Fetch the Latin excerpt from The Latin Library or Splash Latino.
3. Draft the full entry in English following the structure above.
4. Save the Italian translation to `italian_translations_[era].md` in the project folder -- do not include it in the main draft. Italian translations will be added in a second pass.
5. Propose the tier ranking; confirm with the user before finalizing.
6. Save each era as a separate Google Doc.
